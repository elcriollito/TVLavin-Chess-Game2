# Research note: “Who Is Caissa?”

Internal editorial research record. This file is not linked from the public article.

Sources reviewed on 2026-07-23:

- Folger Shakespeare Library catalog, *Scacchia ludus, or, The game of chess. A poem* (1736 English/Latin edition record). Confirms Vida authorship and the established bibliographic identity of the chess poem.
- Google Books, Marco Girolamo Vida, *Scacchia, Ludus: A Poem on the Game of Chess* (1750 translation; digitized from the National Library of the Netherlands). Confirms the work’s title, authorship, and transmission in English.
- Scuola Normale Superiore research repository, Ilaria Ottria, “Ludimus effigiem belli: Gioco e guerra nel poemetto Scacchia Ludus di Marco Girolamo Vida” (2024). Confirms current scholarly treatment of Vida’s poem as a work joining play, war, and classical literary form.
- University of Toronto Libraries, Jackson Bibliography of Romantic Poetry, *Poems, Consisting Chiefly of Translations from the Asiatick Languages* (1772). Library-verified bibliographic record for Jones’s collection.
- Folger Shakespeare Library catalog, Jones, *Poems consisting chiefly of translations from the Asiatick languages* (1772). Confirms authorship, publication details, and British Library source copy.
- Google Books, Jones, *Poems Consisting Chiefly of Translations from the Asiatick Languages* (1772). Digitized primary edition whose contents list “Caissa or The Game of Chess.”
- University of Leeds Library, special-collections record for Jones’s 1772 volume. Independently confirms “Caissa, or, The game at Chess” within the collection.
- Wikisource transcription and scan index for the 1777 edition of Jones’s collection. Consulted only alongside institutional catalog records to review the poem’s broad narrative; no passage is quoted in the article.

Editorial conclusions:

- Caissa must be described as a literary figure, not an ancient Greek or Roman deity.
- Vida is an earlier and influential chess-poetry precedent; the article avoids claiming that Jones merely renamed an identical Vida character.
- Jones’s youthful composition is commonly dated to 1763, while the directly verified collected publication is 1772.
- The article paraphrases the poems and contains no copied passage.
